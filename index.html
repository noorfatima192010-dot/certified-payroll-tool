How to Fill Out Form WH-347: The Ultimate Certified Payroll Compliance Guide

Navigating government compliance on federal or state-funded construction projects can be incredibly daunting. Under the Davis-Bacon Act, contractors and subcontractors are legally required to submit weekly certified payroll records using Form WH-347. A single math error, misclassified worker, or missed filing window can result in severe penalties, frozen contract payments, or audits.

📋 What is Form WH-347?

Form WH-347 is a weekly payroll report mandated by the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) for public works projects. It tracks the exact daily hours, wage rates, and fringe benefits paid to every laborer on the job site to prove adherence to prevailing wage laws.

⏳ Timely Filing Rules

Certified payroll reports must be completed and submitted to the project's contracting agency exactly seven days after the end of each workweek. Delays can freeze funding for the entire project.

Step-by-Step Instructions for Form WH-347 Fields

To properly satisfy audit requirements, ensure your administrative team populates the following fields meticulously:

  • Contractor / Subcontractor Details: State your full legal corporate identity and official address. Make sure to clearly state whether you are the Prime Contractor or a Subcontractor.
  • Project Identification Number: Input the precise federal contract tracking number provided by the hiring agency. Missing this causes immediate system rejection.
  • Employee Personal Identifiers: Enter the full name and only the last 4 digits of the Social Security Number (SSN) for every laborer. Full SSNs should never be included due to privacy compliance laws.
  • Work Classification: State the exact tier of the laborer (e.g., Journeyman Electrician, Apprentice Plumber). The title must match the official local prevailing wage determination sheet perfectly.

Understanding Tiered Daily Math & Overtime Rules

The most common reason for audit failures is incorrect math regarding Straight Time (ST) versus Overtime (OT). Under the Contract Work Hours and Safety Standards Act, any hours worked over 8 hours per day or 40 hours per week must be paid at a minimum of 1.5 times the basic prevailing wage rate.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. What happens if we don't have any work during a specific week?

You must still submit a report! It is called a "No Work" report. You increment the payroll number sequentially, write "No Work" across the employee rows, sign the statement of compliance, and submit it on time.

2. How long are we required to store our historical WH-347 payroll records?

The Department of Labor requires all contractors to securely preserve their certified payroll data and corresponding site records for at least 3 years following the formal completion of the contract.
